[A] Come in, Tom, Priya. Have a seat. So, this is our first meeting about your group project for the Small Business Skills certificate. How are you getting on?
[B] Pretty well, thanks, Dr Marsh. We've had a couple of meetings already.
[C] Yes, and we've finally decided which business we want to study.
[A] Good. Last time you mentioned a café, didn't you?
[C] We did, but when we checked the list on the noticeboard, we found that four other groups had already chosen cafés. So we changed our minds.
[B] We're going to look at the second-hand bookshop on Mill Street instead. The owner is a friend of my uncle, so we thought it would be easier to get access.
[A] That's a sensible choice. It'll make your project stand out, too.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Now, how are you planning to collect your data?
[C] We were going to put an online questionnaire on the shop's social media page. It would be quick and cheap.
[A] Hmm. I can see why that's attractive. But think about who actually shops there. From what I've seen, a lot of the regular customers are retired people. They're much less likely to answer an online survey, so you'd only hear from a small part of the customer base. I'd really recommend face-to-face interviews in the shop.
[B] That makes sense. We hadn't really thought about the age of the customers.
[C] How many people should we interview? We thought twenty would be enough.
[A] Twenty is a bit low to draw any reliable conclusions. I'd say you need at least thirty. More if you can manage it, but thirty is the minimum I'd accept.
[B] Thirty. Okay, that's fine. If we go on two Saturdays, it shouldn't be a problem.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Have you identified any problems with the shop yet?
[B] We've spent some time looking around, and the first thing we noticed is the sign outside. It's very small and faded, and you can hardly see it from the other side of the road.
[C] And the opening hours are quite limited. It closes at four o'clock, and it's not open at all on Sundays, which is when a lot of people go shopping on Mill Street.
[A] What about the prices?
[B] Actually, the prices seem very reasonable compared with other bookshops in town, so we don't think that's an issue.
[C] And some people said the location might be a problem, but Mill Street is really busy, so we don't agree.
[A] Good. Make sure your interview questions test those ideas rather than just confirming them.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Now, how are you going to divide the work?
[C] I'm going to design the interview questions, because I did something similar in my last job.
[B] And I'll be the one who contacts the owner, since I know the family. I'll arrange the dates for the interviews with him.
[A] And the analysis?
[C] We thought we'd do that together. It's the biggest part of the project, and we both want to learn how to use the spreadsheet software properly.
[A] That's a good idea. Now, one important point. Before you interview anyone, you must get written consent from each person. Just a short form they sign to say they agree to take part and understand how the information will be used.
[B] Written consent. Okay, we'll prepare a form.
[pause 2.0s]
[C] Can I ask about the report? How long should it be?
[A] Last year the limit was two thousand five hundred words, but the course team has changed it. The final report must not be more than three thousand words, not including the appendices.
[B] And the presentation?
[A] Each group will present for fifteen minutes. That includes time for questions from the audience, so don't plan to speak for the whole fifteen minutes.
[C] Is there anything you'd recommend we read before we start?
[A] Yes. There's a very practical little book in the library, a guide to questionnaire design. It'll help you avoid questions that push people towards a particular answer.
[B] Great, I'll look for it this afternoon.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Right. Shall we meet again once you've drafted the questions?
[C] Could we come next Tuesday?
[A] I'm away at a conference on Tuesday. How about Thursday morning instead, at ten?
[B] Thursday at ten is fine for me.
[C] For me too. Thank you, Dr Marsh.
[A] You're welcome. Good luck with it.
