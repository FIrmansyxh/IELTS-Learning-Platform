[A] Good morning, everyone. Thank you for joining this short training session. My name is Alan Price, and I work in the occupational health team. Today I'm going to talk about workstation ergonomics, which is simply the science of arranging your desk, chair, and equipment so that your body is comfortable and safe while you work.
[A] You might wonder why the company is spending time on this. Well, last year we looked at the reasons staff gave for taking sick leave. We found that back and neck pain accounted for about one third of all sick days across the organisation. That's a huge number, and most of those problems are preventable with a few simple changes.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Let's start with the screen, because that's where most problems begin. Many people believe the middle of the screen should be at eye level, but that actually makes you tilt your head back slightly. The top of the screen should be roughly level with your eyebrows. That way, your eyes look slightly downwards, which is the most relaxed position for your neck.
[A] Distance matters, too. If the screen is too close, your eyes have to work harder, and if it's too far away, you'll find yourself leaning forward. A good rule is to place the screen about an arm's length away. Sit back in your chair, stretch out your arm, and your fingertips should just about touch the screen.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Next, your chair. Adjust the height so that your elbows are at the same level as the desk when your shoulders are relaxed. Your feet should be flat on the floor. If you're shorter and you can't reach the floor after raising the chair, don't let your feet hang. Ask for a footrest. We have plenty of them in the stores, and they're free to any member of staff who needs one.
[A] I'm often asked about standing desks, which have become very fashionable. Some people think that standing all day is the answer to back pain. It isn't. Standing for long periods brings its own problems, such as tired legs and pressure on the lower back. Standing desks are only helpful if you change position regularly, moving between sitting and standing during the day.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Now, let's talk about your eyes. Staring at a screen for hours can cause dryness, headaches, and blurred vision. Optometrists recommend what's called the twenty, twenty, twenty rule. Every twenty minutes, look at something twenty feet away for twenty seconds. It sounds very simple, but it really does help your eye muscles relax.
[A] Many of you work on laptops, especially those who work from home some days. Laptops are convenient, but they are terrible for posture, because the screen and keyboard are joined together. If the screen is at the right height, the keyboard is too high, and the other way round. So if you use a laptop for more than an hour a day, raise it on a stand and use a separate keyboard and mouse.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] Before rolling this out to everyone, we ran a pilot scheme with two departments, Finance and Customer Services. Over six months, staff in those departments had their workstations assessed and adjusted. I'm pleased to say the results were encouraging. The number of sick days taken because of back and neck pain fell noticeably.
[A] In addition, when we surveyed the staff afterwards, they reported higher job satisfaction than before. Now, I should be honest with you. We did not measure productivity, so I can't tell you whether people actually got more work done. And the scheme didn't save money in the short term, because we had to buy new chairs and monitor stands.
[pause 2.0s]
[A] A quick word about lighting. Glare on the screen is a common cause of eye strain. The best arrangement is to position your desk at right angles to the window, so the daylight comes from the side. Don't sit with the window directly behind your screen, or directly behind you, because either way you'll get too much contrast or reflection.
[A] Finally, if you'd like a professional to check your workstation, you can request an individual assessment. These are carried out by the facilities team, not by occupational health, so please send your requests directly to them. They'll visit your desk, make adjustments, and order any equipment you need.
[A] Right, I'll stop there. Thank you for listening, and I'm happy to take any questions.
