## A Saturday at Green Lane Market

Green Lane Market opens every Saturday morning in the car park behind the town library. It starts at eight o'clock and closes at two in the afternoon. About sixty small stalls sell fruit, vegetables, bread, cheese, flowers and second-hand books. Most of the sellers live within thirty kilometres of the town, and many of them have been coming to the market for more than ten years.

Maya Santos has sold vegetables at the market for six years. She and her husband have a small farm outside the town. On Friday evening they pick the vegetables, and early on Saturday morning they drive to the market in an old van. "I like to talk to the customers," Maya says. "People ask me how to cook a vegetable they have never tried, and I tell them. It makes the work more interesting."

The busiest time is between ten and twelve o'clock. Families walk slowly between the stalls, children try small pieces of cheese, and a young man plays the guitar near the entrance. There is no entrance fee, but the sellers pay the town council eight pounds each week for their place. If it rains heavily, the market stays open, but fewer people come, and some sellers go home early.

Last year the town council asked visitors what they would like to change. Most people said they were happy with the market, but about a third complained about the lack of parking. As a result, the council made an agreement with the primary school across the road: from this spring, visitors can leave their cars in the school car park, which is free on Saturdays. Some people also asked for a cafe, and the council is now planning one. It will not open until next year.

Not everyone buys food. Some visitors come only to listen to the music or to meet friends. A retired teacher, Mr Chen, comes every week to buy flowers for his kitchen table, and then he drinks a cup of tea on a bench by the old fountain. "It is the best part of my week," he says. "I always know I will see someone I know."
