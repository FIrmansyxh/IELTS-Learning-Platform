In many large cities, traffic congestion is a serious problem. Some people believe that governments should invest in public transport rather than constructing additional roads. In my opinion, this is a sensible approach, although roads still have a role to play.

The main reason for supporting public transport is that it reduces the number of vehicles on the street. A single train can carry as many passengers as several hundred cars, so the same amount of money can serve far more people. For example, after the metro system was extended in Jakarta, many commuters stopped driving to work, and the traffic on some main routes became lighter. In addition, fewer cars means less pollution, which is better for public health.

Another advantage is that public transport is more affordable for ordinary citizens. Owning a car involves fuel, insurance, maintenance and parking fees, which many families cannot afford. A reliable bus or rail network gives these people access to jobs, schools and hospitals, and this helps to reduce inequality between different groups in society.

On the other hand, building new roads cannot be ignored completely. Goods are usually delivered by trucks, and people in rural areas often have no alternative to private vehicles. If roads are in poor condition, this can slow down the economy and cause accidents.

In conclusion, I believe that governments should give priority to public transport because it is cleaner, cheaper and more efficient for large populations. However, a limited budget should still be kept for maintaining the existing roads, especially outside the cities.
